You are designing a POSITIONING PRESENTATION DECK for a product called fabricXai — The Garment

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
THE ONE IDEA THE DECK MUST LAND
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
An ERP is only as good as the data people put into it. In a garment factory, the people who hold the
real information — the line supervisor, the merchandiser, the QC inspector, the storekeeper — are
almost never the people who can operate ERP software. That gap is why factory ERPs fail.

fabricXai closes the gap. It is one integrated, AI-powered ERP for garment factories, and it can also
run as an intelligence layer on top of a factory's existing ERP. Its copilots let people with almost
no software skill run and maintain the ERP with almost no effort: they describe what happened in plain
language (or a photo, or a voice note), and the system does the ERP work and asks them to approve it.

The core promise, in one line: "Describe what happened. fabricXai does the ERP. You just approve."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
NON-NEGOTIABLE FRAMING RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. fabricXai is ONE integrated system with a set of features. NEVER present it as a roster/catalogue
   of separate agents or 22 products. If you show modules, show them as FEATURES OF ONE PLATFORM,
   grouped into a few families — under one shell, one login, one copilot.
2. Do NOT show a grid of 22 logos/agents. One product, one story.
3. The hero mechanic is the copilot loop: PLAIN-LANGUAGE INPUT → SYSTEM DRAFTS THE ERP ENTRY →
   HUMAN APPROVES. Make this loop visually central and repeat it as a motif.
4. Speak to two people on every relevant slide: the OWNER (what the business gains) and the
   FLOOR WORKER / STAFF (how little effort it takes them). This owner/worker duality is core.
5. Keep language "Gazipur-simple" in product-experience slides — short, concrete, no ERP jargon
   dumped on the reader. Save the sophistication for the visual design.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
WHAT THE PLATFORM ACTUALLY IS (source material — all real, use freely)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
One integrated web app, one shell and sidebar, one copilot. Features (group them as ONE platform,
in these families — do not enumerate as separate products):

• Sales & Orders: Lead Management (capture, score, campaigns, inbox), Buyer Management (buyer
  profiles, tiers, contracts, health score, feedback & issues), RFQ & Quotation (intake RFQs, build
  quotations, track clarifications), Contacts.
• Costing & Finance: Costing (garment cost sheets — material/labor/overhead/freight — with automatic
  FOB pricing for a target margin), Finance (AR/AP, per-order P&L, cash flow, banking & Letters of
  Credit).
• Supply Chain: Supplier Evaluation (scorecards, RFQ board to suppliers, samples), Inventory
  Management (fabric/trims master, stock ledger/GRN, warehouse, material requests, finished goods,
  reorder & forecasting), Shipment (freight booking, live tracking, export document vault, buyer
  updates, exceptions).
• Production & People: Production Planning (master plan/Gantt, line allocation, Time & Action
  calendar, material shortages, risk view), Workforce Management (roster, attendance & leave, skill
  matrix, training, welfare & safety), Machine Maintenance (registry, preventive planner, breakdowns,
  spare parts, predictive maintenance).
• Quality & Compliance: Quality Control (inline QC with DHU/defects, final QC with AQL sampling, lab
  tests, CAPA, standards), Compliance & Policy (policy library, audits, regulatory monitor),
  Sustainability (ESG dashboards, waste & materials, carbon footprint & Digital Product Passport).
• Intelligence: Analytics & Reporting (cross-module KPI dashboards, AI explainers, report library,
  scheduled reports), role-based Dashboards (MD/CEO, merchandising, production, QC, HR, finance,
  compliance, line supervisor), and a unified Approve inbox.

Real proof points to weave in (these exist in the product):
• Every feature is expressed in OWNER value + WORKER value. Real examples to use verbatim as pairs:
  – Costing: owner "accurate margins" / worker "auto-updated rates & FX"
  – Quality: owner "reduced rework" / worker "AI defect detection & CAPA"
  – Production: owner "reduce late orders" / worker "automated Gantt & alerts"
  – Shipment: owner "higher OTIF" / worker "auto buyer alerts & ETA tracking"
  – Compliance: owner "zero audit failure" / worker "automated CAPA tasks"
  – Lead Mgmt: owner "more RFQs from same outreach" / worker "AI suggests the next touch"
• Real deterministic engine example (use as the "the math is real, the copilot just narrates it"
  proof): the cost sheet computes FOB = totalCost ÷ (1 − margin%). The copilot doesn't guess prices;
  it fills the sheet and the engine computes the number. Same idea for QC (DHU, AQL) and production
  efficiency.
• Real copilot surface: an "Ask MARBIM" affordance across the app opens one AI assistant panel with
  chat, attachments, reasoning steps, tables and charts, and ACTION BUTTONS. Real scripted examples of
  the propose-then-approve voice to quote on slides:
  – RFQ: "I've parsed the RFQ PDF. Found 3 missing specs — fabric weight tolerance, packing method,
    test requirements. Shall I draft a clarification email to the buyer?"
  – Shipment: "Container 8X-12 is delayed 24h due to port congestion in Chittagong. Revised ETA
    March 15. Shall I notify the buyer with updated tracking?"
  – Quality: "Line 2's DHU rose to 4.2% from broken-stitch defects. Likely needle alignment. Recommend
    operator retraining + equipment check."
  – Costing: "Style D-220: material 62%, labor 18%, overhead 20%. Margin dropped on a 7% fabric price
    rise. I found an alternate supplier 4% cheaper."
  These show the copilot DRAFTS and RECOMMENDS, then asks a yes/no — the human approves.
• Real approval model: a single Approve inbox where items (POs, budgets, contracts, leave) each carry
  an AI score and an approval chain — the human-in-the-loop half of the loop.
• Real trust model: role-based access (11 roles: admin, manager, sales, production, finance,
  procurement, compliance, HR, operations, quality, viewer; create/read/update/delete/approve/export
  permissions) and per-company data isolation. Use this for a "your data stays yours" slide.
• Real onboarding model: features activate through a guided Intro → Pricing → Onboarding flow, with
  three tiers (Basic / Growth / Enterprise). Show the platform as something you switch features on for,
  not a monolith you install.
• Real "layer on top of your ERP" hooks: import from CSV/Excel/CRM (Salesforce/HubSpot mentioned),
  upload PDF contracts and RFQs, log phone/email/WhatsApp communications. Present as: fabricXai reads
  your existing data and adds the intelligence + easy-input layer on top.
